AI Readiness
Is the government ready to use AI?
▾
AI Readiness measures whether a country has the government strategy, technology sector, and data infrastructure needed to actually deploy AI in public services. A high score means a country has a national AI strategy, a skilled digital workforce, and the data systems to make AI work. A low score means these foundations are missing or deeply underdeveloped — no matter how much political enthusiasm exists.
Source: Oxford Insights Government AI Readiness Index 2024 — a peer-reviewed annual benchmark used by UNESCO and the G20, covering 40 indicators across 188 countries (CC BY-SA 4.0).
🌐
AI Access
Can ordinary people actually use AI?
▾
AI Access measures whether everyday people can actually reach and use AI tools — not just whether the internet technically exists. It factors in how affordable data is, whether AI tools work in local languages, and whether political restrictions block access to global platforms like ChatGPT or Claude. A country with fast internet but no AI tools in its language scores lower than connectivity data alone would suggest.
Source: A4EAI composite score — ITU 2024 connectivity data, Alliance for Affordable Internet, GSMA Mobile Intelligence, and Freedom House internet freedom ratings.
⚖️
AI Governance
Who has a say in how AI is governed?
▾
AI Governance measures whether a country has real rules around AI — and whether citizens and civil society have a genuine voice in shaping those rules. Importantly, this dimension applies a penalty for countries that deploy AI for mass surveillance or authoritarian control. Having an AI strategy on paper means very little if it serves repression rather than people.
Source: A4EAI composite — OECD AI Policy Observatory, UNESCO Readiness Assessment Methodology, Oxford GARI governance pillar, Freedom House, and the AI Now Institute.
🔬
Co-creator Index
Is this country building AI or just using it?
▾
The Co-creator Index is A4EAI's most distinctive measure. It asks: is this country actually producing AI — building models, publishing research, shaping global standards — or simply consuming AI tools built entirely by others? The Global South is increasingly incorporated into AI as a market and a data source, while being excluded from authorship. This index makes that structural divide visible.
Source: A4EAI composite — Stanford HAI AI Index 2024, CSET research database, Epoch AI model registry, and ISO/IEC JTC1 AI standards participation records.
